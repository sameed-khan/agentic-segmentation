# Research: Locally deployable visuospatial VLMs for 2D-rendered 3D MRI tumour segmentation

**Cutoff note.** The request says “late 2026.” Primary materials retrieved include releases through 9 September 2026 (BiomedParse v2 repository). I cannot establish that this is the final set of late-2026 releases; no claim of exhaustive coverage beyond this evidence. Benchmarks below are authors’/model-card results on their own tasks, not MRI tumour localization results. **No candidate has published a validated score on the harness’s 169-case task.**

## 12-line summary
1. Best first experiment: use the frontier agent as-is and compare **GUI-Actor-2B** and **Molmo2-8B** as isolated point/region proposals on the identical 169 axial slices.
2. GUI-Actor-2B is the most hardware-safe dedicated visual pointer: 2.23B weights, published ScreenSpot-Pro 36.7, MIT-tagged checkpoint; it predicts regions via a custom head, not standard function calls.
3. Molmo2-8B is the most compelling general-purpose pointer: trained for points, supports multiple images, Apache-2.0; use a coordinate parser and treat weights’ training-data non-commercial restrictions seriously.
4. Qwen3-VL-8B is the best single open-model full-agent experiment: visual-agent/tool and spatial-grounding claims, Apache-2.0, 8.77B; 4-bit is the safer A10 fit.
5. GUI-specialized ScreenSpot scores are only transfer evidence: UI widgets are not enhancing tumours, edema, vessels, or anatomy in MRI.
6. BiomedParse v2 is the strongest medical direct-mask/localization candidate: 3D MRI support, text prompts, built-in object-existence detection, 4.46-GB checkpoint; research/development use only.
7. BiomedParse’s repo explicitly says it reasonably segments only targets learned in pretraining; MRI tumor-specific reliability on this harness is unverified.
8. MedGemma 1.5-4B is a lightweight medical image explainer / possible mask judge, not a segmentation model; Google says it is not evaluated/optimized for multi-turn applications.
9. M3D-LaMed-Phi-3-4B is a useful 3D medical VLM benchmark candidate, but expects a preprocessed volume (1×32×256×256), not the harness’s rendered screenshots.
10. UI-TARS-1.5-7B is worth a small pointer transfer test, but its own 49.6 ScreenSpot-Pro result is far below 61.6 for the non-public larger UI-TARS-1.5.
11. Keep every model as a separately evaluated localizer/judge first; no evidence supports replacing the agent or changing production segmentation from published benchmark scores.
12. Suggested order: GUI-Actor-2B → Molmo2-8B → Qwen3-VL-8B → BiomedParse v2 → MedGemma 1.5 → M3D-LaMed → UI-TARS-1.5.

**Memory arithmetic:** dense BF16 raw weight floor is roughly `2 × parameter-count` bytes; 4-bit raw storage roughly `0.5 × parameter-count` bytes before scales/metadata. Both are researcher estimates, not measured VRAM; vision activations, KV cache, CUDA context, and fragmentation increase actual usage. GUI-Actor 2B and MedGemma 4B are the safest LLMs to attempt BF16 alongside nnInteractive; even they need an actual peak-memory check. Do not assume simultaneous residency—load/unload or reserve a dedicated GPU process if necessary.

### Ranked first tests (six experiments)

1. **GUI-Actor-2B as a pointer tool** — Same 169 cases, same largest GT axial slice/1000px panel and prompt; return its predicted region and center; primary metric hit rate within the GT tumor, plus center distance and invalid-output rate. Compare to Sonnet 5.5 raw-coordinate 0.704 and SoM 0.826. Strength: explicit region grounding at low memory. Weakness: GUI domain shift; custom inference/head.
2. **Molmo2-8B as a pointer tool** — Identical images, tumor instruction, parse points; report hit rate, center distance and sensitivity to panels/SoM. Compare same baselines. Strength: point supervision and multi-image affordance. Weakness: no medical fine-tuning; large BF16 raw memory; license/data restrictions; point fidelity on MRI untested.
3. **Qwen3-VL-8B as full agent and pointer** — Quantize to 4-bit; first test exact multi-turn sequence on 20 cases (three views, numbered marks, `seg_point`/`seg_box`, `seg_view`, `seg_finish`) with strict tool-schema validation; then 169-case localization. Measure tool-call parse success, steps, localization hit, Dice, GPU peak. Strength: strongest plausible general-purpose agent stack. Weakness: local server/version and `pi` multi-image/tool behavior not established by model card.
4. **BiomedParse v2 as direct-mask and independent localization baseline** — Run all 169 3D scans if checkpoint/GPU fits; text queries “soft-tissue tumour” and appropriate tumor terminology; report per-case Dice, sensitivity, empty-mask/false positive, then derive GT-largest-slice hit/box IoU. Do not pass its mask as ground truth. Strength: direct 3D MRI/text-mask design. Weakness: domain labels/training target mismatch; tool-using agent not supported; model’s own results on these cases unknown.
5. **MedGemma 1.5-4B as blinded judge** — On a held-out subset, show slice + overlay with randomized mask identity and ask for target identity, under/over-segmentation, and confidence; compare each response to expert adjudication and boundary metrics, not an LLM-only gold label. Strength: medical vocabulary and low parameter count. Weakness: no established mask-quality benchmark, multi-turn caveat, terms/access.
6. **M3D-LaMed-Phi-3-4B as a volume-native baseline** — Run its official volume preprocessing and referring segmentation on a subset; compare 3D Dice, then screenshot its selected region only if export supported. Strength: 3D medical context. Weakness: significant preprocessing/custom-code friction and not the same rendered-slice/tool regime. **UI-TARS-1.5-7B** follows as optional; use quantized specialist-only pilot, not first full-agent trial.

**Evaluation guardrails:** retain case-level paired results; report hit rate and target-structure error separately; match prompts, resolution, views, and access to context. Avoid tuning on test cases. Add bootstrap confidence intervals and subgroup breakdown by tumor size, anatomic location, modality/sequence, and confusing adjacent anatomy. Assess judge false acceptance of bad masks and false rejection of good masks. Existing reported baselines are user-supplied context (not externally verified here): Sonnet 5.5 0.704 raw-coordinate, 0.826 SoM, overall mean Dice 0.69–0.74.

### Other requested families / what not to select now

- **SpatialVLM:** primary paper is PaLM 2-E-derived and not a locally downloadable open checkpoint; therefore not a runnable candidate despite its spatial reasoning relevance ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12168)). **No open-weight local inference evidence verified.**
- **GUI-Actor-7B/Qwen2.5-VL variant:** card reports ScreenSpot-Pro 44.6 (47.7 with verifier), stronger than 2B; its 7B BF16 weight floor ~14 GB leaves too little shared A10 margin. Choose the 2B model first. 4-bit quantized model not verified.
- **UI-TARS-72B / Qwen3-VL 32B dense / other 30B-class dense:** not suitable at BF16 on the free VRAM. At 4-bit, 72B raw weights ≈36 GB; 32B ≈16 GB before overhead. Qwen3-VL 30B-A3B’s low active count does not shrink weights: ~15 GB raw 4-bit plus vision/runtime means it is not a safe 16–18 GB shared-GPU target. Arithmetic estimates, not benchmark claims.
- **VILA-M3, RadFM-14B, Lingshu/HuatuoGPT-Vision, SpatialRGPT, RoboPoint, OS-Atlas, ShowUI, UGround, AGUVIS, Qwen-VL grounding variants, InternVL3.x, GLM-4.xV, Kimi-VL, MiniCPM-V, Gemma 3/4, Llama 4, Pixtral:** not individually evaluated in this brief because primary-source model-version/license/server/weight details for each were not fetched. Do **not** interpret omission as a finding that they are inferior or unavailable. Their candidacy needs checkpoint-level source verification; API/model names alone are insufficient.
- **MedSAM2 / BiomedParse:** segmentation systems rather than agentic VLMs. BiomedParse v2 is the better-aligned independent 3D text-mask baseline here. Do not conflate that with nnInteractive tool orchestration.
- **MedVol-R1 (MICCAI 2026):** paper describes 2D axial box evidence anchors plus MedSAM2 propagation for CT-ORG, AbdomenCT-1K and KiTS23; its conclusion identifies MRI as future work. It is not evidence for MRI tumor fit, and local weights/server readiness were not verified ([paper](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026/paper/2593_paper.pdf)).

## Contradictions

- **UI-TARS score attribution:** official card distinguishes the downloadable 7B (ScreenSpot-Pro 49.6, OSWorld 27.5) from the larger full UI-TARS-1.5 (61.6, 42.5); some third-party summaries assign the larger scores to the 7B. This brief follows the card’s scale-comparison table.
- **BiomedParse domain generalization:** official v2 says it covers MRI and text-driven 3D tasks; an HF user issue reports difficulty reproducing BraTS tumor-label Dice. The issue is not a controlled model evaluation and does not overturn the model’s documented MRI support, but it highlights that broad MRI support does not prove tumor-mask label agreement. [Official repo](https://github.com/microsoft/BiomedParse/tree/v2); [reproduction issue](https://github.com/microsoft/BiomedParse/issues/117).
- No comparable MRI tumour localization benchmark or results were found for GUI-Actor, Molmo2, Qwen3-VL, UI-TARS, MedGemma, or M3D-LaMed.

## Missing evidence / residual risks

- Peak VRAM with nnInteractive, latency/throughput, quantized accuracy, exact vLLM/SGLang model-version compatibility, and `pi` tool-call interoperability have not been measured.
- The provided 169-case baselines and Sonnet 5.5 results come from the task brief; they were not independently fetched or validated.
- Model-card/author benchmark scores are not apples-to-apples across datasets, prompting, image sizes, or evaluators. ScreenSpot-Pro and spatial-VQA numbers are transfer signals only.
- BiomedParse checkpoint’s actual in-memory VRAM, checkpoint licensing details beyond its repo’s research/development notice, and tumor-specific training coverage remain unverified.
- Clinical use is outside this recommendation: these are research comparisons, and no claim of clinical safety or diagnostic suitability is made.
