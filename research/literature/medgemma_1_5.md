# MedGemma 1.5 for harness v3: evidence-led integration plan

**Scope.** Literature/primary-source research only. No weights were downloaded; no software installed or run. “Verified” below means the statement was checked against the linked primary source(s), not independently reproduced. Suggested system behavior and evaluation are explicitly researcher recommendations/inferences.

## 15-line summary
1. MedGemma 1.5 is one instruction-tuned, multimodal 4B checkpoint; Google describes it as Gemma 3 architecture plus a 400M MedSigLIP encoder.
2. Google lists 896×896 image normalization, 256 visual tokens/image, ≥128K context and up to 8,192 output tokens.
3. Release/model version in Google’s card: 13 January 2026 / 1.5.0; weights are under HAI-DEF terms, not Apache 2.0.
4. The reported MRI training addition is 167,674 examples from internal axial multi-parametric head, abdomen and knee MRI; Google gives no MRI percentage of total training.
5. MRI volumes are represented as ordered 2D axial images, not a native 3D tensor; the report sampled at most 85 slices per training/evaluation query.
6. Reported MRI Dataset 1 macro accuracy is 64.7% (MedGemma 1.5) versus 51.3% (MedGemma 1 4B), on internal binary condition classification—not tumour segmentation.
7. No published evaluation found for our five soft-tissue tumour histotypes, mask completeness, tumour rims, 3D tumour localization, or nnInteractive candidate regions.
8. Bounding-box coordinates were prompted as JSON `[y0,x0,y1,x1]` normalized to [0,1], for 2D frontal chest-X-ray anatomy on Chest ImaGenome—not MRI lesions.
9. VQA-RAD barely changed versus MedGemma 1; SLAKE regressed, so generic medical-VQA scores do not validate the target decisions.
10. Google’s official notebook covers CT, while its serving source also handles DICOM CT/MRI and injects `SLICE n` text; no official NIfTI-to-input example was verified.
11. Use ordered PNG slices from NIfTI after explicit orientation, intensity scaling and labels; that is a harness-side adaptation, not a Google-validated NIfTI path.
12. A community Unsloth GGUF repo lists Q4_K_M (2,489,894,976 bytes) and F16 mmproj (851,252,224 bytes); neither is an official Google conversion.
13. llama.cpp documents Gemma 3 multimodal, image-capable server and function-calling routes; MedGemma 1.5 compatibility and Q4 vision-quality impact remain unbenchmarked here.
14. A single A10 24GB appears plausible for 4B Q4/F16-mmproj at modest context and one request, but actual VRAM/latency must be measured; no A10 results were found.
15. Pilot first as a read-only reviewer/localizer with strict JSON and abstention; keep Claude/nnInteractive in control and evaluate on a frozen, case-level held-out test set.

## Findings

### 1. Model, inputs, release and license

- **Claim:** MedGemma 1.5 4B is Google’s updated Gemma 3-based decoder-only multimodal instruction model; the technical report specifies a 400M MedSigLIP image encoder, and says its encoder was frozen while the language decoder received additional training. **Sources:** [Google model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [technical report, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.
- **Claim:** Google specifies text+vision input, text output, 896×896 normalized images encoded as 256 tokens each, context ≥128K and output up to 8,192 tokens. The official pipeline/processor examples pass messages with `{type:image,image:...}` and `{type:text,text:...}`. **Sources:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [HF checkpoint card](https://huggingface.co/google/medgemma-1.5-4b-it). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Claim:** The Google card identifies model version 1.5.0 and model-created date 13 January 2026; the card says 1.5 is available only as 4B multimodal IT. MedGemma 1 has other 27B variants, but do not mistake these for a 1.5 27B checkpoint. **Sources:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [MedGemma 1 card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card-v1). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Claim:** Weight use is governed by [HAI-DEF Terms of Use](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms), not Apache 2.0. The model card says outputs are not intended to directly inform diagnosis, patient management, treatment recommendations or direct clinical practice; developers must validate/adapt/meaningfully modify for intended use. HAI-DEF restrictions and any distribution obligations require legal review before sharing a service or derivative. **Sources:** [terms](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/terms); [model card, Intended use/Limitations](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high. **Practical implication (inference):** constrain this prototype to research/decision support, clinician oversight and offline evaluation; do not present outputs as clinical determinations.

### 2. Training data and volume input

- **Claim:** The 1.5 report’s table of *additional* training data gives MRI Dataset 1 as 167,674 examples (continued pretraining, distillation and RL), from an internal US outpatient network; axial multi-parametric studies from head, abdomen and knee. **Sources:** [technical report, Table 1 and §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1); [model card, data description](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Claim:** The report’s MRI preprocessing turns volumes into sequences of axial 2D images, rescales each to 896×896 and caps a query at 85 slices during training and evaluation (21,760 visual tokens). MR voxel intensities are relative: report says per-volume min–max normalization, replicated identically across RGB; no CT-style HU windows. Training MRI series included T1w, T2w, GRE and SWI. **Source:** [technical report, §2.3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Important boundary:** 85 is a reported training/evaluation cap, not documented as the hard inference maximum. The model card advertises a long context, but the report does not validate arbitrarily large stacks. Slice selection/downsampling beyond the report recipe needs local validation. **Support:** source interpretation. **Confidence:** high.
- **Claim:** Google’s notebook index calls `high_dimensional_ct_hugging_face.ipynb` an example of prompting MedGemma 1.5 with a 3D CT representation; it is CT-specific. Google’s serving code recognizes DICOM CT and MRI volume sources and automatically injects textual `SLICE n` indicators between images. **Sources:** [official notebook index](https://github.com/Google-Health/medgemma/blob/main/notebooks/README.md); [official predictor.py](https://github.com/Google-Health/medgemma/blob/main/python/serving/predictor.py). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **NIfTI:** no official NIfTI loader/path or NIfTI notebook was verified. **Inference/recommendation:** NIfTI can be adapted by the harness: honor affine/orientation; choose an axial view and document RAS/LPS convention; rescale/crop/pad each 2D slice to square; apply a documented per-volume MRI normalization; replicate greyscale to RGB; label each image `SLICE n` and stack order. Send a pilot small/selected slice set or up to 85 slices, not a NIfTI object. This follows the documented 2D-image interface, but exact NIfTI preprocessing equivalence to Google is unverified.
- **MRI share:** 167,674 is the reported *additional MRI dataset example count*, not a share/percentage of total pretraining. Neither source gives a total training denominator or MRI fraction. No reliable MRI percentage can be computed.
- **Task mismatch:** report’s internal MRI test spans brain infarct/hemorrhage/MS, knee meniscal tears/fractures and abdomen liver disease/pancreatic lesions. It does not name our five soft-tissue tumour histotypes or tumour mask labels. The report’s text does not establish that such cases are absent from all proprietary training, only that no task-specific evidence is reported.

### 3. Relevant benchmark results and localization semantics

- **MRI:** internal MRI Dataset 1 macro accuracy: 64.7% for MedGemma 1.5 4B vs 51.3% for MedGemma 1 4B, Gemma 3 4B 51.1%, and MedGemma 1 27B 57.4%. It is a balanced internal binary condition-classification evaluation, not segmentation, tumor contour, histotype classification or localization. **Sources:** [model card, imaging evaluation table](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [report, Table 3 / §3.2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **3D CT context (secondary):** CT Dataset 1 macro accuracy 61.1% vs MedGemma 1 58.2%; CT-RATE validation macro F1 27.0% vs 23.5%. These are not MRI/tumour evidence. **Sources:** same card/report. **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Localization:** Chest ImaGenome anatomy bounding-box mean IoU 38.0% (model card labels IoU; report table says mean IoU; the report compares 3.1% for MedGemma 1 4B). The evaluation is frontal chest X-ray anatomy, with text request for JSON objects and `[y0,x0,y1,x1]` normalized to [0,1], (y0,x0) top-left. It is not a dedicated tumour-box test. **Sources:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [report, §3.2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **VQA-RAD:** tokenized F1 48.1 (MedGemma 1.5) vs 49.9 (MedGemma 1); closed-subset accuracy 70.2 vs 69.1. **SLAKE:** model card gives tokenized F1 59.7 vs 72.3; closed-subset accuracy 82.8 vs 87.6. These suggest no broad VQA uplift and SLAKE regression; they do not test outline quality/completion. **Source:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Reported discrepancy:** technical report’s result table is indexed as SLAKE F1 59.8 in one passage, while Google’s model card says 59.7. Treat the 0.1 point as table/version/rounding discrepancy; use neither as exact reproducibility evidence. **Sources:** [report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1); [card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card). **Confidence:** medium.
- **VQA-RAD / SLAKE populations:** report describes mixed radiology images and default or contamination-avoiding splits, but these datasets do not validate soft-tissue MRI rim acceptance. **Source:** [report evaluation methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** high.
- **No-mask / segmentation evidence:** the sources above report text answers, classification and boxes, not pixelwise segmentation, mask Dice, lesion contour completeness, rim recall, or nnInteractive candidate acceptance. A text-only VLM should be tested as an advisory reviewer, not assumed to act as a segmentation model. **Support:** source-based negative finding. **Confidence:** medium-high.

### 4. Prompting and harness roles

- **Official prompt/template evidence:** Google’s Transformers example constructs user messages containing image then text, calls `processor.apply_chat_template(messages, add_generation_prompt=True, tokenize=True, return_dict=True, return_tensors="pt")`, and uses `model.generate(..., max_new_tokens=2000, do_sample=False)`. Report Appendix B says MedGemma models do not have a system instruction; evaluation prompts are task-specific. The card cautions that MedGemma may be more prompt-sensitive than Gemma 3. **Sources:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [report Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Advice (inference):** avoid assuming Claude-style system-message behavior transfers; put concise task instructions and output contract in the user message, use fixed deterministic decoding initially (`do_sample=False` / low randomness where engine supports), and compare prompt variants only on the design split. Use JSON fields + explicit `uncertain/abstain`; parse and validate rather than asking it to call nnInteractive. Google’s 2,000-token example is not a requirement; use short completion (e.g., 128–256 tokens) for review output and measure truncation.
- **(a) Localizer — proposed:** give oriented slice(s) with numbered marks, tumour histotype (as a provided label, not diagnosis request), side/series metadata; request `{lesion_mark_id, slice_id, confidence, alternatives, abstain}`. Primary endpoint: top-1 and top-k mark accuracy; also wrong-structure rate, lesion visibility abstention precision/recall, and per-histotype sensitivity. Prefer selecting among existing marks to inventing free coordinates. If requiring box output, use Google’s observed normalized y,x order only as a starting convention and map/test against exact panel dimensions; it was learned/evaluated on chest X-ray anatomy, not these MRI panels.
- **(b) Mask judge — proposed:** provide consistently rendered slices with green current contour and unmasked MRI; ask `complete / incomplete / uncertain`, specific slice/mark and missed-vs-excess contour location. Do not accept free-text as coordinates. Endpoints: sensitivity to incomplete masks (especially missed rims), false-complete rate (primary safety endpoint), specificity on complete masks, localization of defect to slice/region, abstention and inter-reader agreement. Evaluate raw images and overlay variants separately; overlays/colored outlines are a harness-specific domain shift with no official evidence.
- **(c) Candidate judge — proposed:** each candidate region labeled A/B/... on identical baseline image; ask accept/reject/uncertain and reason (tumour vs non-tumour, rim vs artifact). Endpoints: candidate-level precision/recall, F1, false-rejection rate, tumour-rim recall, per-case “all valid candidates retained”, sensitivity by histotype/size/slice. Compare with current Claude decision, nnInteractive baseline heuristics, and blinded expert labels. Never have the reviewer mutate masks automatically during the first pilot.
- **(d) 3D reviewer — proposed:** send labelled, ordered axial samples from one sequence per series; multimodal user message supports multiple image entries in standard Transformers. Start with a small representative set; add slices if context/memory allow. Do not upload a flattened montage as a substitute without a separate experiment: image tokens/coordinates and mark readability change. Report’s max-85 and 256 tokens/image imply 21,760 vision tokens at 85 slices, before textual prompt, but that is not a guarantee of performance on every 85-slice case.
- **Role priority (inference):** start with (a) mark selection and (c) candidate review because both can be converted to discrete, auditable choices; assess (b) false-complete risk before giving any “done” influence. Treat volumetric input as later ablation—quality, context cost and slice sampling remain unknown.

### 5. Local deployment and alternatives

- **GGUF availability:** the community [Unsloth MedGemma 1.5 GGUF repository](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/medgemma-1.5-4b-it-GGUF/tree/main) identifies Google’s model as base and lists Q4_K_M at 2,489,894,976 bytes (~2.32 GiB) and `mmproj-F16.gguf` at 851,252,224 bytes (~0.79 GiB). These are Hugging Face file sizes, not measured resident VRAM. Its metadata identifies HAI-DEF terms. This is community-converted, not an official Google GGUF. **Support:** direct repo/API listing. **Confidence:** high.
- **Repository status:** no official Google GGUF was found; this research verified Unsloth only. No specific bartowski or LM Studio community checkpoint/file size was verified, so do not rely on those names without rechecking. Q4_K_M quantizes the language model; the chosen mmproj is F16 by file label. No Q4-vs-BF16 MedGemma 1.5 image-quality comparison was found, so quality preservation is unknown.
- **llama.cpp:** current [multimodal docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md) document Gemma 3 multimodal via `-m` plus `--mmproj` and `llama-server -hf` for listed supported GGUFs; [server docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md) document OpenAI-compatible chat completions, image URL/base64 content, and `--mmproj`; [function-calling docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/function-calling.md) describe tool calls with `--jinja`. This establishes the server mechanics, not that this exact community MedGemma checkpoint is tested or that the model’s tool-call quality is reliable. Verify startup and one-image/one-multiple-image correctness locally before harness integration.
- **Transformers/BF16 alternative:** Google’s card supplies `AutoModelForImageTextToText` `torch_dtype=torch.bfloat16`, `device_map="auto"` and processor examples. Google’s FAQ reports approximate static weight memory for 4B as BF16 6.4 GB, SFP8 4.4 GB, Q4_0 3.4 GB; these are weights only, not activation/KV/runtime. This suggests the stated 20 GB free A10 can plausibly run BF16 with modest context and one request, but no precise 85-slice runtime guarantee. **Sources:** [card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card); [official FAQ](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/faqs). **Confidence:** high for published static estimates; medium for hardware fit inference.
- **vLLM alternative:** vLLM docs list Gemma 3 multimodal support and an image processor, while Google documents vLLM deployment as an option; neither source verifies MedGemma 1.5 on this exact A10/software setup. Prefer Transformers as the clearest Google-local reference path; vLLM is a serving alternative to validate, not an assured drop-in. **Sources:** [vLLM Gemma 3 multimodal docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/api/vllm/model_executor/models/gemma3_mm/); [vLLM supported models](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/models/supported_models/); [Google FAQ](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/faqs).
- **VRAM and latency:** no measured A10 (24 GB), CUDA 12.8, harness-panel or multi-slice latency data were located. At 4B Q4, modest 8K context, one request, the stated GPU seems feasible from published static weights plus file sizes, but this is a researcher inference; image encoder workspace, KV cache, batch, server build and OS reservation matter. Measure warm/cold latency, peak `nvidia-smi` VRAM, token/image throughput and OOMs at 1/4/8/16/85 images. Start one concurrent request.

**Suggested exact llama.cpp launch (not executed):** run from an existing CUDA-enabled llama.cpp build after manually placing the two community files in a local directory; do not mix mmproj from another model.
```bash
MODEL_DIR=/models/medgemma15
llama-server \
  --model "$MODEL_DIR/medgemma-1.5-4b-it-Q4_K_M.gguf" \
  --mmproj "$MODEL_DIR/mmproj-F16.gguf" \
  --n-gpu-layers 99 --ctx-size 8192 --parallel 1 --jinja \
  --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8088
```
`--ctx-size 8192` is a conservative pilot setting, not a benchmark-optimal value. If this exact checkpoint fails image parsing, do not infer general Gemma3 support means compatibility; test same-repo mmproj, current llama.cpp version and GGUF conversion provenance. No download or launch command was run.

**OpenAI-compatible image request shape (send local PNG as base64):**
```json
{"model":"medgemma-1.5-4b-it-Q4_K_M.gguf","messages":[{"role":"user","content":[{"type":"text","text":"Review mark 4 on this MRI slice. Return JSON only: {\\\"choice\\\":\\\"lesion|not_lesion|uncertain\\\",\\\"reason\\\":\\\"short\\\"}. Do not infer beyond the image."},{"type":"image_url","image_url":{"url":"data:image/png;base64,<BASE64_PNG>"}}]}],"temperature":0,"max_tokens":128}
```
Use a unique image label in text for each slice; the server docs support base64 `image_url`. Tool calls are not needed for a read-only reviewer; use the existing Claude agent for nnInteractive tool execution. Local JSON is advisory and must pass schema/enum validation.

### 6. Limits, evidence on fine-tuning, recommended evaluation

- **Claim:** Google explicitly says MedGemma has not been evaluated or optimized for multi-turn applications; prompt sensitivity may exceed Gemma 3. Intended-use and validation limits appear above. **Source:** [model card](https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medgemma/model-card). **Support:** direct. **Confidence:** high.
- **Fine-tuning/localization:** the report documents adding Chest ImaGenome 39,968 training examples with bounding boxes in RL and evaluates chest-X-ray anatomical boxes; this is in-model training, not evidence that an external fine-tuned checkpoint improves MRI tumour localization. I found no primary, peer-reviewed study establishing MedGemma 1.5 fine-tuning for soft-tissue MRI tumor boxes, segmentation, mask completion or candidate rims. **Source:** [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05081v1). **Support:** direct on training/evaluation; absence statement limited to sources searched. **Confidence:** high on what report covers; medium on negative literature finding.
- **Design-set protocol (recommendation/inference):** group splits by patient and preferably acquisition/site; stratify five histotypes and lesion size/site. Keep prompt examples, thresholds, image scaling, output schema and decision thresholds on a design split only; freeze code/model/prompt before opening held-out cases. Never split slices/candidates from one patient across train/design/test. Compare current Claude-only harness, MedGemma-only advisory, Claude with MedGemma suggestion, and simple nnInteractive baseline. Blind annotators to model identity; adjudicate disagreements.
- **Metrics:** role (a): top-1/top-k localization, per-histotype wrong-structure rate, abstention calibration. Role (b): incomplete-mask sensitivity and false-complete rate (primary), complete-mask specificity, defect localization, rim-specific miss rate. Role (c): candidate accept/reject precision/recall/F1, candidate false rejection, rim recall, case-level all-valid-region recall. All roles: patient-level bootstrap CIs, subgroup results, calibration/abstention coverage, latency, VRAM, crashes and tool-independent reproducibility. Report raw counts and confidence intervals, not only aggregate averages.
- **Prompt protocol:** on design split, pre-register a small grid of instructions (task phrasing, labels, examples/no examples, overlay color conventions), fixed output schema, and decoding settings. Select once by the safety-weighted metric (especially false-complete/false-reject) and freeze. Evaluate one-shot / repeated-call stability and 1/4/8/16/all-slice policies. Do not tune from held-out outputs. Save full image ordering, prompt/version, raw response, parser result and decision trace.
- **Human/agent role:** initial pilot must be read-only and non-authoritative. A language-model judgement is not an anatomical truth; use expert labels and current Claude/nnInteractive behavior as baselines. A favorable pilot does not establish clinical safety or regulatory readiness.

## Contradictions

- SLAKE tokenized F1 is 59.7% in the Google model card versus 59.8 in a technical-report table extraction. Likely rounding/version difference; retain the card value and disclose discrepancy.
- MedGemma 1.5 localizer capability is described broadly as anatomical localization, but the described coordinate task and benchmark are specifically 2D frontal chest-X-ray structures. Do not generalize that claim to MRI tumour masks.
- Google advertises 3D CT/MRI interpretation, while its report’s mechanism is multiple axial 2D slices; “3D support” is volume-as-slice-sequence, not a native 3D image encoder.

## Missing evidence

- No MRI-specific 1.5 NIfTI notebook or exact supported NIfTI ingestion route verified; only DICOM volume handling code and 2D image examples.
- No performance on five soft-tissue tumour histotypes, nnInteractive overlays/candidates, completeness decisions, rim regions, segmentation metrics, or multi-turn harness workflows.
- No verified per-model A10/CUDA 12.8 VRAM, latency, batching or 85-slice runtime result; no GGUF quantization quality comparison.
- No verified Google-official MedGemma 1.5 GGUF; the checked Q4_K_M is community Unsloth.
- No reported percentage of all training data devoted to MRI. Proprietary training composition may be undisclosed.
- No localization-specific downstream fine-tuning evidence for MRI soft-tissue tumors was verified; official box localization training is chest-X-ray anatomy.
