## 15-line summary
1. MedSAM2 is a medical fine-tune of **SAM2.1-Hiera-Tiny**, not a native 3D encoder; it reads slices as video frames.
2. Its paper's standard volume workflow is one **2D box on the middle/key slice**, then propagation in both directions.
3. It has meaningful MRI evidence, including liver MRI lesions, but no verified MSTT, T1/T2 sarcoma, or 169-case evaluation.
4. The paper reports median Dice 88.37% (IQR 79.91–93.26) on its MRI-lesion holdout; this is not a prediction for MSTT.
5. MRI preprocessing in the paper clips foreground intensity at the 0.5th/99.5th percentiles and rescales to [0,255]; it does not prescribe CT windowing for MRI.
6. Input is resized from SAM2's 1024 to 512 pixels; 600×600 slices therefore require resizing and some spatial-detail trade-off.
7. Official repo documents `medsam2_infer_3D_CT.py` and video inference; it does **not** document a dedicated 3D MRI CLI in the fetched README.
8. Official HF card says weights are restricted to research and education; check downstream dataset/model terms separately.
9. Exact MedSAM2 runtime, GPU memory, 600×600×300 throughput and A10/A100 fit are **not established** by the fetched paper/repo sources.
10. SAM-Med3D and SegVol are genuine volume-native alternatives; SegVol has positive/negative points, boxes and text, while SAM-Med3D is chiefly point/mask prompted.
11. VISTA3D is a native 3D point-interactive candidate but its published model is CT-focused; it is a poor MRI-first choice.
12. PRISM is the closest documented prompt-interface match (3D clicks, box, scribbles, iterative mask feedback); target MRI/MSTT transfer is unverified.
13. MedSAM3 (2025) is a SAM3-based medical concept/video adaptation, not evidence of a 3D-native volume model; Medical SAM3 is a separate 2026 project.
14. SAM 3D is a natural-image 3D reconstruction model, not a 3D MRI segmentation alternative; “BiomedParse v2” was not verified.
15. Recommendation: run **MedSAM2 as the named SAM2-family swap**, and add **PRISM (or SAM-Med3D/SegVol)** as a clearly separate native-3D sensitivity arm; do not claim tool-equivalent prompting without reporting the adapter.

## 1. MedSAM2: architecture and volume workflow

- **Direct:** MedSAM2 is developed by full-model fine-tuning of the lightweight **SAM2.1-Tiny** checkpoint. Architecture is Hiera image encoder + prompt encoder + memory-attention module + mask decoder. Each slice/frame is encoded in 2D; memory attention conditions a frame on previous frames' features/predictions. The standard 3D protocol prompts the middle slice with a bounding box and propagates both directions to the volume ends. [1, 2]
- **Direct:** Image input was reduced from 3×1024×1024 to **3×512×512**. The paper says outputs are produced at 128×128 then bilinearly upsampled to 512×512. Thus it is 2D slice/video propagation, not volumetric 3D convolutions or joint 3D attention. [1]
- **Prompts:** The paper says the prompt encoder can encode points, boxes and masks, but boxes are the main prompt; its tested volume benchmark uses a middle-slice box. Its annotation pipeline lets a human revise the 2D key-slice mask, then propagates the refined mask across the volume. The paper explicitly lists support for point/text/scribble/lasso as future expansion. **Interpretation:** SAM2's base predictor has prompt-update primitives, but that does not establish that all prompt modalities or persistent multi-slice corrections are a supported, validated MedSAM2 product workflow. In particular, do not advertise native scribble/lasso interaction. [1, 2]
- **Negative points/multiple slices:** Not established as a MedSAM2 paper result. SAM2-style point labels permit foreground/background point input at the underlying predictor level, and prompts can be associated with frames; the fetched MedSAM2 paper's evaluated 3D method remains box-on-key-slice propagation. Treat repeated key-slice corrections and negative points as an **adapter capability to test**, not as verified MedSAM2 behavior. [1, 2]
- **Input normalization (paper):** CT is modality-windowed (listed brain/abdomen/bone/lung/mediastinum windows), then rescaled to [0,255]. MRI and PET instead use foreground-intensity cutoffs at the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles, then rescale to [0,255]. No universal MRI window or T1/T2-specific normalization is specified in the cited methods. [1]
- **Spatial geometry:** Slice-as-time reduces use of volumetric geometry to slice ordering and 2D spatial features. **Inference:** Input orientation/axis and spacing must be fixed identically across cases; wrong axis, flipped order or reorientation alters the propagation problem. Because this cohort is 1-mm isotropic, the through-plane-spacing mismatch problem is less severe than in anisotropic scans, but resampling/resizing remains necessary for 512-pixel input. This is engineering inference, not a paper claim.

## 2. MedSAM2 evidence, checkpoints, deployment, caveats

- **MRI evidence (direct):** The paper's holdout covers 40 segmentation tasks; MRI lesions are 318 cases aggregated across five tasks. MedSAM2's MRI-lesion median DSC is **88.37% (IQR 79.91–93.26)**. It also reports iterative annotation of 3,984 liver MRI lesions (including T2-weighted examples) with progressively fine-tuned rounds. It does not test MSTT-199 or establish performance for soft-tissue tumour MRI generally. [1]
- **Comparison context (direct):** Holdout evaluation used bounding boxes on middle slices and bidirectional propagation. Its specialist baseline EfficientMedSAM-Top1 has median 82.25% on MRI lesions; MedSAM2's result is from a different trained generalist system, not an apples-to-apples model swap on our test set. [1]
- **Official entry points (direct):** Official GitHub README exposes a 3D CT lesion command `python medsam2_infer_3D_CT.py -i CT_DeepLesion/images -o CT_DeepLesion/segmentation`, video entry point `medsam2_infer_video.py`, Gradio `app.py`, and a CT RECIST inference path. The fetched README does not list an `infer_3D_MRI` script. MRI weights exist, but that is not proof of an official MRI volume CLI. [2]
- **Checkpoints (direct):** Official README/download script and HF card identify `MedSAM2_2411.pt`, `MedSAM2_US_Heart.pt`, `MedSAM2_MRI_LiverLesion.pt`, `MedSAM2_CTLesion.pt`, and `MedSAM2_latest.pt`; MRI liver-lesion checkpoint is task-specific, `latest` is general-purpose per project materials. Verify checkpoint/config pairing before evaluation. [2, 3]
- **License (direct):** HF model card says model weights may be used **only for research and education**. The official code repository is open, but code license and weight license are distinct; the restriction on model weights rules out treating them as generally commercial-use weights. Dataset licenses remain separate. [2, 3]
- **Runtime/VRAM:** The paper documents training batch of eight slices/frames per GPU and a smaller 512 input, but no reproducible inference-time, peak-VRAM, or A10/A100 measurement for the 600×600×300 scenario was found in fetched primary sources. **Unverified:** any statement that it runs at a particular speed or fits a 24-GB A10. Full-volume frame caching/propagation could be memory- and latency-sensitive; that is an engineering risk to profile, not a published result.
- **Paper-stated limitations:** boxes are the main prompt, and this can limit thin/branching complex structures. The model uses fixed eight-frame memory; the paper says this can harm tracking with rapid/large motion or targets that disappear/reappear. It is 2D slice/frame processing, does not explicitly model 3D continuity, and requires GPU computation. Reported limitations primarily discuss video; assess volumetric failure modes independently. [1]
- **Practical mismatch:** MRI test evidence is mostly liver lesion/MRI-organ, not MSTT. T1/T2 target appearance, tumour heterogeneity, slices with necrosis/edema, and large fields of view could challenge the model; these are **risks/inference**, not failures proven by its paper.

### Apples-to-apples published comparisons

- **nnInteractive paper benchmark:** against static prompts, nnInteractive reports relative gains of **10.1 Dice points** over the closest 2D competitor for points, **14.9** over SAM2 for boxes, and **23.8** for scribbles. Paper notes 2D competitors could receive more prompts (e.g. one point per slice), and compares on its own test/OOD datasets, not MSTT. Its paper includes SAM-Med3D and SegVol in the 3D baselines; VISTA3D and PRISM were excluded because the authors describe them as closed-set designs, so this is not a complete cross-model ranking. [14]
- **nnInteractive challenge:** official README reports first place in the CVPR 2025 Interactive 3D Segmentation Challenge. No challenge leaderboard score was verified in this pass; do not report an invented numeric score. [15]
- **MedSAM2's 88.37% MRI lesion median Dice** is on its own holdout, not head-to-head with nnInteractive or PRISM and not MSTT. Do not compare this number directly with nnInteractive's different tasks/prompts. [1, 14]
- **2026 correction-aware paper:** an open Springer paper reports a multi-prompt 3D tumor refinement method evaluated on MSD-Colon and KiTS21, positioning PRISM as its closest matched baseline and describing iterative points/3D box/scribbles/mask feedback. These are CT benchmarks, not MRI MSTT; no cross-paper rank is justified. [5]

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Primary requested swap: MedSAM2**, if the research question is specifically “what changes when replacing nnInteractive with a leading SAM2-family medical model?” It has the most relevant reported MRI-lesion evidence among candidates checked, and a clear single-key-slice box + bidirectional propagation protocol. Keep nnInteractive as unchanged control; explicitly label MedSAM2 as **different prompt semantics** rather than equivalent support for the same complete toolset.
2. **Best workflow-fidelity candidate: PRISM**, conditional on confirming its official implementation/checkpoint, license, expected input size, and one-GPU VRAM. It natively matches 3D click/box/scribble/mask-refinement behavior more closely, but is not demonstrated on MSTT MRI and is an older, task-specific benchmark model. If harness/tool parity is the priority, PRISM may be a fairer interactive comparator than MedSAM2.
3. **Native-3D foundation sensitivity arm:** SAM-Med3D or SegVol. Pick SAM-Med3D for native 3D point/mask prompting; SegVol if 3D boxes + negative points + optional text are useful. Expect prompt API mismatch (no direct lasso/scribble) and domain shift because the cited training emphasis is not MSTT T1/T2.
4. **Avoid VISTA3D as the MRI primary** unless a verified MRI checkpoint is supplied. Keep Medical SAM3/MedSAM3/VoxTell as exploratory, gated follow-up candidates, not as settled winners.
5. To make claims robust, run all selected checkpoints frozen, document provenance and license, test memory and latency on one representative largest volume, and lock preprocessing/prompt sequences before the 169-case run. Do not fine-tune on test cases or use test references to pick checkpoints/prompts.

**Methodological interpretation:** “Same agent + same 169 cases” alone is not sufficient for apples-to-apples if one model receives only a key-slice box while another receives scribbles/lasso/3D box. Primary analysis should preserve the exact agent, renders, coordinates, tool call budget and turn count, but publish both (a) a **common-denominator prompt protocol** accepted by each model and (b) a **native-capability protocol** with all model-appropriate tools. Report conversion/loss of information per call, and present metrics per interaction count and cumulative latency. No test-time target tuning.

## 5. Session adapter: capability mapping

The rows below describe a proposed adapter, not existing MedSAM2 features. Native refers to a source-documented or base-model primitive; emulated means the interaction is transformed, with information loss.

| Harness action | MedSAM2 mapping | Fidelity / required adapter behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `set_image(3D)` | Load oriented NIfTI; normalize MRI foreground percentiles; convert selected slice axis to ordered 2D frame sequence; resize each frame to model's 512×512 input. | **Native sequence input, emulated volume geometry.** Preserve affine and exact inverse resize so return mask matches original voxel grid. The published 3D CLI is CT-focused; MRI inference path must be written/validated. |
| `seg_point` positive / negative | Add labeled point(s) on selected frame/key slice using SAM2 prompt update API; propagate both directions. | **Base-SAM2 primitive; MedSAM2-tested workflow not established.** Keep labels, frame ID and coordinates in state. Do not silently convert negative points to positive. Validate correction persistence and propagation. |
| `seg_box` 2D | Apply box on given slice/keyframe, propagate forward/backward. | **Closest to native published protocol.** This is preferred common-denominator protocol. |
| `seg_box` 3D | Use an agreed representative slice (e.g., center slice and cross-section box) or prompt a box independently on several slices. | **Emulated/lossy.** Do not claim it is a native volumetric box. Multi-slice boxes change prompt count and propagation behavior; log each frame box. |
| `seg_scribble` positive/negative | Option A: sample labeled points along stroke; Option B: rasterize 2D scribble/region into mask prompt where the predictor supports mask input. | **Emulated.** Sparse-point conversion loses contour continuity; mask-input conversion needs resolution mapping and confirmation of expected mask semantics. Count original scribble as one action and disclose point count. |
| `seg_lasso` closed 2D contour | Fill polygon into a 2D binary mask on that slice; submit as mask prompt, then propagate. Fallback: bounding rectangle. | **Emulated.** Filled mask is the closest representation; rectangle loses shape and is a much weaker prompt. Never silently substitute one for the other. |
| `seg_refine` / initial mask | Take the provided full 3D mask's chosen key slice as a mask prompt; optionally add selected slices as further keyframes, then bidirectionally propagate. | **Partially native at frame level; whole-volume mask-refinement not verified.** Mask downsampling/interpolation and slice-selection policy must be fixed before evaluation. |
| `seg_paint` direct mask edit | Apply deterministic voxel edit to returned mask and store as external correction; optionally feed edited slice mask back to predictor state. | **Not model-native.** To retain parity, same direct-edit tool remains available for every model; distinguish model output from external edited output. Rebuild/reseed memory if edits are fed back. |
| `seg_edit` morphology | Apply same deterministic morphology to output mask, same parameters and voxel units in every arm. | **External postprocessing, not a prompt.** Freeze operations/radius and use physical spacing (1 mm here); account for tool budget consistently. |
| `reset`, `undo`, `checkout` | Reset predictor state and replay interaction history from selected checkpoint/branch; or cache predictor states if deterministic and supported. | **Adapter/session behavior.** Model state mutation means undo must be replay-safe. Keep immutable prompt ledger, seed and checkpoint. |
| Accumulating prompts / full mask each turn | Store all prompts with order/label/slice. On each new key-frame correction, update state then propagate through the entire stack in both directions; ensure output full-resolution 3D mask at every tool return. | **Needs harness implementation and validation.** Recompute may be expensive; stale propagated masks and contradictory prompts must be resolved deterministically. Measure latency per interaction, not just per volume. |

**Fairness controls:** identical image orientation, 1-mm grid, intensity scaling policy where feasible, view/renders and marks, agent/system prompt, tool availability, allowed calls/tokens/time, case order and stopping rule. Use equal external edits in both arms; log calls, points sampled from scribbles, seeded slices, retries, wall-clock time, VRAM peak and failed cases. Report “native” and “emulated” actions separately; do not count an emulated many-point scribble as cost-free. Preserve full trajectory for audit.

## 6. Engineering / validity risks and unverified items

- A 600×600×300 volume is 108M voxels before intermediate tensors. MedSAM2 consumes a sequence of resized 512² frames, not a 512³ volume. **No source-verified** A10/A100 runtime/VRAM result for this exact scale was found; benchmark once with representative data before committing.
- If using 2D frame resizing, assess whether the longest axis is compressed; document interpolation and inverse mapping. Percentile normalization must be per-volume and foreground-defined consistently for T1 and T2. Avoid case-specific hand-selected windows.
- Medical checkpoint mismatch and target overlap: establish training-data provenance/possible test leakage for MSTT before interpreting performance. The MedSAM2 paper's reported MRI-lesion data are not shown to equal independent soft-tissue-tumour cohorts.
- Prompt semantics can alter quality drastically: one center box is not equivalent to repeated point corrections, filled lasso mask, or sampled scribble points. Report matched protocol and tool-native protocol separately.
- License is a deployment constraint: MedSAM2 HF weights say research/education only. Verify each alternative's **code, checkpoint and training-data** terms independently before distributing or using outside research/education.
- No independently verified evidence in this research pass for a “BiomedParse v2” release, a specific late-2026 nnInteractive successor, universal A10-fit, or MedSAM2 MRI CLI. These remain **unverified**, not negative claims of nonexistence.
